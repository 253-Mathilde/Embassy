New York City is the most populous and linguistically diverse metropolis in the United States, home to over 8.5 million residents who speak more than 800 languages. Founded by Dutch settlers in 1624 as New Amsterdam, the British renamed it New York in 1664. The city even served briefly as the nation’s first capital from 1789 to 1790, hosting George Washington's inauguration. Today, the city is a global titan of culture, finance, and infrastructure. It operates one of the world's largest public transit networks with 472 subway stations running 24/7.Beneath the streets of Manhattan, the Federal Reserve Bank guards the world’s largest gold vault, storing roughly 25% of the globe's gold bullion. New York is also defined by its iconic landmarks and unique urban lore. The Statue of Liberty was gifted by France and assembled on-site from 350 individual pieces, while the massive expanse of Central Park is almost entirely man-made. Local culture is so distinct that it even has its own economic tracker: the Pizza Principle, a decades-old economic phenomenon where the price of a standard slice of cheese pizza historically mirrors the cost of a single subway ride.
